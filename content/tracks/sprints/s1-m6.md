## Goal, and why it matters

Estimate roll and pitch from your IMU in real time, and *show* why fusion works: on one live plot, the accelerometer-only angle is noisy, the gyro-only angle drifts, and the fused angle is both smooth and stable.

Sensor fusion is on nearly every robotics job description, and the complementary filter is the version you can explain on a whiteboard in two minutes: **trust the gyro for fast changes, trust gravity for the long run.** It's also the gateway to the Kalman filter in Module 6. ## Step 1: what each sensor is good and bad at

| | Accelerometer | Gyroscope |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | specific force: gravity **plus** any acceleration | angular **rate** (°/s) |
| Gives an angle by | trigonometry on the gravity vector | integrating rate over time |
| Short term | noisy, and wrong whenever the IMU accelerates (a bump, a vibration) | smooth and accurate |
| Long term | correct on average (gravity doesn't drift) | **drifts**: any bias integrates into a growing error |
| In signal terms | good at low frequencies | good at high frequencies |

That last row is the whole idea. A complementary filter is a **low-pass filter on the accelerometer angle** plus a **high-pass filter on the gyro angle**, with the same crossover frequency, so the two "complement" each other back to a flat response. If you've designed a crossover for a two-way speaker (woofer gets the lows, tweeter gets the highs), you already understand it.

## Step 2: angles from gravity

With the IMU still, the accelerometer measures gravity only. Using the axis convention printed on your board:

$$\text{roll} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y, a_z) \qquad \text{pitch} = \operatorname{atan2}\left(-a_x, \sqrt{a_y^2 + a_z^2}\right)$$

Roll is rotation about X, pitch is rotation about Y. Use `atan2`, not `atan(ay / az)`: `atan2` gets the quadrant right and doesn't divide by zero when the board is on its side. The units of `a` don't matter (g or raw counts), because only the ratio does. Yaw (rotation about Z) is impossible from gravity: gravity looks the same whichever way you face. That's why phones also have a magnetometer.

## Step 3: gyro bias

A still gyro doesn't read 0. It reads a small offset, the **bias**, typically a few °/s on an MPU-6050, different for each axis and each chip, and it changes with temperature. Integrate a 1 °/s bias and your angle is off by **60° after one minute**.

The simple calibration: hold the IMU still for a few seconds, average each gyro axis, and subtract that average from every later sample. Two details make it robust:

- **Check that it was actually still.** If the standard deviation of the gyro during calibration is much larger than the noise you see when it's sitting on the desk, someone bumped it. Reject the calibration and ask again. (Find your threshold from a log of the IMU sitting still.)
- **Let it warm up.** The bias moves as the chip warms up in the first minutes. Log the temperature too, so you can see this in 1.7.

## Step 4: the complementary filter

Each new sample, with time step `dt`:

$$\theta_k = \alpha \left(\theta_{k-1} + \omega_k \Delta t\right) + (1 - \alpha) \theta_{\text{acc},k}$$

- `θ_{k-1} + ω_k·dt` is the **gyro prediction**: last angle plus rate times time.
- `θ_acc,k` is the accelerometer angle from Step 2.
- `α` (alpha), between 0 and 1, sets the blend. `α = 1` is pure gyro integration; `α = 0` is pure accelerometer.

**What alpha means physically.** The filter's time constant is

$$\tau = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{1 - \alpha}$$

With `α = 0.98` and `dt = 0.01 s`, τ ≈ 0.49 s: disturbances on the accelerometer faster than about half a second are rejected, and gyro drift slower than that is corrected. The crossover frequency is 1/(2πτ) ≈ 0.3 Hz.

**Why the bias still matters with the filter.** With a constant uncorrected gyro bias `b`, the filter doesn't drift forever, but it settles at a steady error of about `b·τ`. A 1 °/s bias with τ = 0.49 s leaves a permanent 0.5° error. Calibrate *and* filter.

**Sign check, on your hardware.** The gyro axis you integrate must turn the same way as the accelerometer angle. Tilt the board so roll increases (accel-only roll goes up). Does `gx` go positive during the motion? If it goes negative, the filter fights itself. Find out on your board before writing any filter code. (Using `gx` as the roll rate and `gy` as the pitch rate is a small-angle approximation; it's fine for tilts under about 30° to 45° at a time. The exact version uses the Euler angle kinematics from your mechanics course.)

## `imu_filter.py`, the spec

Write `host/imu_filter.py`. numpy is allowed. Names and signatures must match.

### `estimate_gyro_bias(gyro) -> float or numpy array`

`gyro` is a sequence or numpy array of still-gyro samples: shape `(N,)` for one axis, or `(N, 3)` for three axes. Return the mean along the samples: a single number for one axis, an array of 3 for three axes. Raise `ValueError` if there are no samples.

### `accel_angles(ax, ay, az) -> tuple[float, float]`

Return `(roll_deg, pitch_deg)` in **degrees**, from the formulas in Step 2. Inputs are numbers in any consistent unit.

### `complementary_step(angle_prev, gyro_rate, accel_angle, dt, alpha=0.98) -> float`

One update of the filter. Angles in degrees, `gyro_rate` in °/s (bias already removed by the caller), `dt` in seconds. Raise `ValueError` if `dt <= 0` or `alpha` is outside [0, 1].

### `run_complementary(gyro_rates, accel_angle_series, dt, alpha=0.98, angle0=None) -> numpy array`

Run the filter over two equal-length 1-D series (raise `ValueError` if they're not). Return an array of the same length where element `k` is the estimate **after** processing sample `k`. The starting angle (the "previous" angle for sample 0) is `angle0`, or the first accelerometer angle if `angle0` is `None`. Reuse `complementary_step`; don't write the equation twice.

## Step 5: the live plot

The structure that works well with Matplotlib (see [plotting a CSV log](#/t/sprints/bg-plot) for the drawing side):

- Keep the last few seconds of data in fixed-length buffers: `collections.deque(maxlen=500)` is 5 s at 100 Hz, and old samples fall off automatically.
- In each screen update (aim for about 20 updates per second, **not** 100), read **all** the bytes waiting on the port, feed them to your `PacketParser`, run every new sample through the filter, append to the buffers, then redraw the three lines once.
- Redrawing on every sample is the classic mistake: drawing takes longer than 10 ms, the serial buffer backs up, and `dropped` climbs. Your counters will tell you if this happens.

Plot the three estimates of the same angle on one axes, with a legend, a fixed y-range (for example -90° to +90°), and the parser counters in the title.

## Common beginner mistakes

- Alpha on the wrong term: `α·θ_acc + (1 − α)·(gyro prediction)`. The result is almost pure accelerometer: noisy, and useless during motion. There's a test for it.
- Forgetting `dt`: adding °/s straight to degrees.
- Mixing radians and degrees: `math.atan2` returns **radians**.
- Integrating `gx` for pitch (wrong axis), or integrating it with the wrong sign (see the sign check).
- Computing `dt` from the host's clock instead of the Uno's `t_ms`. The host clock jitters by milliseconds; the device's timestamps don't.
- Calibrating the bias once and hard-coding it. Every power-up and every chip is different.

```
run_complementary(gyro, acc, dt, alpha, angle0):
    check gyro and acc are 1-D and the same length, else raise ValueError
    out = new array, same length
    angle = acc[0] if angle0 is None else angle0
    for k in each index:
        angle = complementary_step(angle, gyro[k], acc[k], dt, alpha)
        out[k] = angle
    return out

live plot, each screen update:
    chunk = all bytes waiting on the port
    for each sample from parser.feed(chunk):
        convert units; subtract the bias
        roll_acc = accel roll angle
        roll_gyro = roll_gyro + gx * dt
        roll_fused = complementary_step(roll_fused, gx, roll_acc, dt, alpha)
        append all three to their buffers
    set the line data from the buffers; redraw once
```

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fused angle moves the wrong way during a tilt, then creeps back | Gyro sign opposite to the accel angle | Do the sign check; flip the sign of that rate. |
| Fused angle is as noisy as the accelerometer | Alpha applied to the wrong term, or alpha too small | Check the equation; try 0.98. |
| Fused angle lags a lot and drifts slowly | Alpha too close to 1, or bias not removed | Calibrate the bias; try a lower alpha. |
| Gyro-only angle drifts even right after calibration | Normal: noise integrates into a random walk. Large drift means a bad calibration (moved during it) | Check your stillness test; see 1.7 for why some drift is unavoidable. |
| Angle is off by a factor of about 57 | Radians vs degrees | Convert once, at one place. |
| Angles are fine flat, crazy near 90° | Pitch formula using `atan(ax/az)`, or roll and pitch both near 90° (gimbal lock) | Use `atan2`; keep test tilts moderate. |
| Live plot freezes, or `dropped` climbs | Redrawing too often, or reading one frame per update | Update about 20 times per second and read everything waiting. |
| Plot window is blank, or not responding | Never yielding to the GUI event loop | Use `FuncAnimation` or `plt.pause()` (see bg-plot). |
