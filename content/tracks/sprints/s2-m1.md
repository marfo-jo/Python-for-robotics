## Goal, and why it matters

A safe, instrumented rig whose firmware does four jobs on a strict 10 ms schedule: **count** encoder edges (interrupts), **drive** the motor from serial commands (PWM + direction), **protect** itself (a command watchdog), and **report** count, current, bus voltage, and temperature.

Interrupts, `volatile`, atomic reads, quadrature decoding, PWM, H-bridges, and fail-safe design are the core of embedded interviews. After this milestone each one is something you built and debugged, not something you read about.

## Wiring

![Motor rig wiring: 12 V supply through a 2 A fuse into INA219 VIN+, VIN- to L298N +12V, star ground at the supply negative, L298N OUT1/OUT2 to the motor, ENA/IN1/IN2 to D9/D7/D8, encoder A/B to D2/D3, INA219 SCL/SDA to A5/A4, DS18B20 DQ to D4 with a 4.7k pull-up](content/tracks/sprints/img/motor-rig.svg)

**Power path (heavy wire, 18 to 20 AWG):**

| From | To | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 12 V supply + | 2 A fuse | fuse as close to the supply as possible |
| 2 A fuse | INA219 VIN+ | the INA219 is on the **high side**: between the supply and the load |
| INA219 VIN− | L298N +12V | all motor current flows through the INA219's 0.1 Ω shunt |
| 12 V supply − | L298N GND | the motor current's return path; the supply's − terminal is the **star ground** |
| L298N OUT1, OUT2 | motor M+, M− | twist the pair; a 0.1 µF ceramic capacitor across the motor terminals |

**Logic (jumpers):**

| Uno pin | Connects to | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 5V | INA219 VCC, encoder VCC, DS18B20 VDD (red), one end of the 4.7 kΩ | the Uno's 5V powers **only** logic |
| GND | INA219 GND, encoder GND, DS18B20 GND (black), and the star ground | one logic-ground wire back to the supply's − terminal |
| D2 (INT0) | encoder channel A (often labeled C1) | external interrupt pin |
| D3 (INT1) | encoder channel B (often labeled C2) | the Uno's only other external interrupt pin |
| D4 | DS18B20 DQ (yellow) + the other end of the 4.7 kΩ | 1-Wire data with its pull-up to 5 V |
| D7 | L298N IN1 | direction |
| D8 | L298N IN2 | direction |
| D9 | L298N ENA | PWM speed (remove the ENA jumper first). D9 is on Timer1, so `analogWrite` gives about 490 Hz |
| A4 (SDA) | INA219 SDA | I2C, address 0x40 with A0 and A1 unbridged |
| A5 (SCL) | INA219 SCL | |
| (none) | L298N +5V | **leave unconnected**. Never connect it to the Uno's 5V. |

Why D9 for PWM? D2 and D3 are taken by the encoder (only they have external interrupts), and D5/D6 run on Timer0, which `millis()` also uses. D9 keeps the PWM independent of your timebase.

**Encoder wire colors vary by seller.** Many JGA25-370 encoder boards are labeled `M1 GND C1 C2 VCC M2` (or `M+ M-`, `A B`). M1/M2 are the **motor** (12 V side), and the other four are the **encoder** (5 V side). Mixing them up puts 12 V PWM on your encoder or on a Uno pin. Identify them from the label or the listing, and check with the multimeter before powering anything.

## Safety

- 12 V supply **off** while wiring. USB first, 12 V last; 12 V off first.
- Fuse on the 12 V positive lead, at the supply.
- Clamp the motor. Nothing on the shaft but a coupler or a small flag of tape to see rotation.
- Never connect the L298N's +5V or the 12 V supply to the Uno. Never run the motor from USB power.
- Keep the 12 V supply switch within reach: it's your emergency stop.
- The L298N heatsink gets hot under load. Don't touch it during or right after tests.

## Bring-up: one subsystem at a time

Like commissioning a machine, bring the rig up one subsystem at a time, so when something fails you know which part failed.

### 1. Encoder, with 12 V off

**Quadrature.** A Hall encoder outputs two square waves, A and B, 90° apart. Read together, the pair steps through a Gray code: in one direction `AB = 00 → 01 → 11 → 10 → 00`, and in the other direction the same sequence backwards. Exactly **one** bit changes per step.

- Every edge on A **and** B is one count: that's **x4 decoding**, the highest resolution you can get.
- The **direction** comes from the order. Compare the previous `AB` state with the new one: a step forward in the sequence is +1, backward is -1.
- If **both** bits changed at once, you missed an edge (noise, or edges faster than your ISR). Count these as invalid transitions; it's a free health monitor.

**Resolution.** Counts per output-shaft revolution = encoder pulses per motor revolution (often 11 for these Hall encoders; check your listing) × 4 (x4 decoding) × the gear ratio. With 11 pulses and a 1:34 gearbox that's about 1496 counts per output revolution. **Measure it** rather than trusting the listing: put a tape flag on the shaft, turn it exactly 10 turns by hand, and divide the count by 10.

**Interrupt load.** Assume the motor shaft (before the gearbox) spins at about 10,000 rpm at 12 V with no load. That's 10,000 / 60 × 11 × 4 ≈ **7300 edges per second**, one every 137 µs on average. An ISR using `digitalRead` takes a few µs, so it's comfortably fast, but it must be **short**: no `Serial`, no floating point, no loops.

**The C rules that matter (interview classics):**

- The count is shared between the ISR and `loop()`, so declare it **`volatile`**. Without it, the compiler may keep a copy in a register inside `loop()` and never see the ISR's updates.
- A 32-bit `long` takes **four** instructions to read on the 8-bit AVR. If an interrupt fires halfway through, you get two halves of two different values (a "torn read"). Copy it with interrupts briefly disabled: `noInterrupts()`, copy to a local variable, `interrupts()`.
- Use `attachInterrupt(digitalPinToInterrupt(pin), isr, CHANGE)` for both pins, and `pinMode(pin, INPUT_PULLUP)` (many Hall encoder outputs are open-collector).

Test: print the count twice a second while you turn the shaft by hand. Check the sign, that the invalid count stays 0, and measure CPR.

### 2. INA219, with 12 V on and the motor disconnected or stopped

The INA219 measures the voltage across its 0.1 Ω shunt (the current) and the voltage on VIN− (the bus voltage). Registers are **16 bits, big-endian (MSB first)**. To read one: write the register pointer, then read 2 bytes. To write one: send the pointer, then the MSB, then the LSB.

| Pointer | Register | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| `0x00` | Configuration | reset value **`0x399F`** (use this as your "who am I" check) |
| `0x01` | Shunt voltage | **signed** 16-bit, **10 µV per LSB** |
| `0x02` | Bus voltage | bits 15:3 = voltage, **4 mV per LSB**; bit 1 = CNVR (conversion ready); bit 0 = OVF (math overflow) |
| `0x03`, `0x04`, `0x05` | Power, Current, Calibration | only meaningful after you program Calibration; you don't need them |

Current from the shunt register: I = (shunt_raw × 10 µV) / 0.1 Ω, so **1 LSB = 0.1 mA**, and the ±320 mV range is ±3.2 A. Doing this conversion on the host keeps the firmware simple and the log raw.

Configuration fields (`0x00`):

| Bits | Field | Reset value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | RST | 0 | write 1 to reset |
| 13 | BRNG | 1 | bus range: 0 = 16 V, 1 = 32 V |
| 12:11 | PG | 11 | shunt range: ±40, ±80, ±160, **±320 mV** |
| 10:7 | BADC | 0011 | bus ADC: 12-bit, one sample |
| 6:3 | SADC | 0011 | shunt ADC: 12-bit, one sample |
| 2:0 | MODE | 111 | shunt and bus, continuous |

**PWM ripple.** The motor current is chopped by the 490 Hz PWM. A single 532 µs shunt conversion catches a random point in the ripple, so readings jump around. The INA219 can **average** in hardware: setting SADC to `1100` averages 16 samples over 8.5 ms, about four PWM periods, which fits your 10 ms tick and gives the mean current. (`1011` = 8 samples, 4.26 ms; `1101` = 32 samples, 17 ms.) Work out the new configuration value from the bit table, write it, and read it back.

The bus voltage (VIN− to GND) also tells you whether the supply is sagging under load. Log it in every test.

### 3. DS18B20

Use the `OneWire` (Paul Stoffregen) and `DallasTemperature` (Miles Burton) libraries: in the Arduino IDE, Sketch > Include Library > Manage Libraries, search for each, Install. The library does the 1-Wire protocol; you do the timing design.

**The trap:** a 12-bit conversion takes up to **750 ms**, and by default `requestTemperatures()` **waits** for it. That freezes your 100 Hz loop for three-quarters of a second, the watchdog fires, and the encoder count stops being sampled. Use the library's non-blocking mode (`setWaitForConversion(false)`): request a conversion, keep running your loop, and read the result about 750 ms later; then request the next one. Temperature changes slowly, so one reading per second is plenty.

Reading the result over 1-Wire still takes a few milliseconds, with interrupts briefly disabled for each bit. Measure it with `micros()` before and after, and make sure your 10 ms tick still has slack. Look up the sensor's address once in `setup()` and read it by address: searching the bus by index on every read is slower.

Two values mean "something's wrong," not a temperature:

- **85.00 °C**: the power-on default; you read before any conversion finished.
- **-127.00 °C**: no sensor answered (wiring, or the pull-up is missing).

Tape the probe flat against the motor can, not the gearbox.

### 4. The motor, at low PWM

The L298N's truth table for channel A:

| ENA | IN1 | IN2 | Motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| PWM | HIGH | LOW | runs one way, speed set by the duty cycle |
| PWM | LOW | HIGH | runs the other way |
| HIGH | same level | same level | **brake** (motor terminals shorted through the bridge) |
| LOW | any | any | **coast** (free-wheel) |

`analogWrite(9, duty)` with `duty` from 0 to 255 sets the duty cycle. A signed command maps naturally: the sign picks IN1/IN2, the magnitude is the duty. Note that the L298N drops about 2 V, so at 100% the motor sees about 10 V, and below some duty (often 15% to 30%) the motor won't start at all: that's the **deadband** (static friction). You'll measure it in 2.2.

## The command protocol (host to Uno)

ASCII lines ending in `\n`, at 115200 baud:

| Command | Effect |
|---|---|
| `PWM <n>` | `n` is an integer from -255 to 255; sign = direction. Out of range: reject it, reply `# ERR <reason>`, change nothing. |
| `STOP` | duty 0 |
| `PING` | does nothing except keep the watchdog alive |

**Watchdog:** if no valid command arrives for **500 ms**, set duty 0 and print `# WATCHDOG` once. The host's job is to send a command (or `PING`) at least every 100 to 200 ms during a test. This is the same idea as a PLC's communication-loss fault response or a fieldbus heartbeat: the safe state must not depend on the host behaving.

**Parsing without blocking.** Never use `Serial.parseInt()`, `readString()`, or `readStringUntil()` here: they wait up to a 1-second timeout, which wrecks your 10 ms schedule. Instead, each loop, move whatever bytes `Serial.available()` reports into a small character buffer; when you see `\n`, terminate the string, handle the command, and clear the buffer. Guard against overflow: if the buffer fills without a newline, throw it away and reply `# ERR`.

## The stream format (Uno to host)

One header line, comment lines starting with `#`, then at 100 Hz:

```
t_ms,pwm,count,shunt_raw,bus_raw,temp_c
```

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `t_ms` | unsigned | `millis()` at the sample |
| `pwm` | int, -255..255 | the duty **actually applied** (0 after a watchdog stop, whatever the last command was) |
| `count` | signed 32-bit | **cumulative** encoder count |
| `shunt_raw` | signed 16-bit | INA219 register `0x01` |
| `bus_raw` | unsigned 16-bit | INA219 register `0x02`, unmodified |
| `temp_c` | float, 2 decimals | the latest DS18B20 reading |

**Why send the cumulative count, not a per-tick difference?** If a line is lost, a cumulative count loses nothing: the next line's count is still right, and the host's speed estimate just uses a longer interval. Deltas would lose those counts forever. It's a small design choice that shows you've thought about lossy links.

**Timing budget (do this yourself and put it in the README):** the worst-case line is about 50 bytes, so about 4.3 ms at 115200 baud. Two INA219 register reads at 100 kHz take roughly 0.5 ms each. The DS18B20 read is the wild card: measure it. Everything must fit in 10 ms with slack, and you should count overruns as in Sprint 1.

<details><summary>Hint 2 (approach)</summary>

ISR: read A and B into a 2-bit state; compare with the previous state you stored in a `static` or global; if it's the next state in the Gray sequence add 1, if the previous state subtract 1, if both bits changed increment the invalid counter; store the new state. Use the same ISR for both pins.

`loop()`: every call, service the command buffer (non-blocking) and check the watchdog (`millis()` minus the time of the last valid command). On each 10 ms tick: copy the count with interrupts off, read the two INA219 registers, service the DS18B20 state machine (request, or read if 750 ms have passed), and print one line.

</details>

<details><summary>Hint 3 (pseudocode)</summary>

```
ISR (on any change of A or B):
    new = (A << 1) | B
    if new is the state after prev in 00,01,11,10: count += 1
    else if new is the state before prev: count -= 1
    else if new != prev: invalid += 1
    prev = new

serviceCommands:
    while bytes are available:
        c = read one byte
        if c is newline: terminate buffer; handle(buffer); clear buffer
        else if buffer is full: clear buffer; print "# ERR line too long"
        else: append c

handle(line):
    if line starts with "PWM ": parse the integer; if -255..255: apply it, last_cmd = now; else print "# ERR range"
    else if line is "STOP": apply 0; last_cmd = now
    else if line is "PING": last_cmd = now
    else: print "# ERR unknown"

each loop:
    serviceCommands
    if now - last_cmd > 500 and pwm != 0: apply 0; print "# WATCHDOG"
    if a 10 ms tick is due: sample everything; print the line
```

</details>

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The Uno resets (header reappears) when the motor starts or reverses | Motor noise or supply dips coupling into the Uno's ground or 5 V | Star ground; twist motor leads; 0.1 µF across the motor; keep logic wires away from motor wires; never share the motor's ground return with the Uno. |
| Laptop says "USB device not recognized" when the motor runs | Ground loop or noise current flowing through the USB cable | Same fixes; make sure motor current can't return through USB. Try a powered USB hub. |
| Count only goes up, or jumps by 2 | Only one channel wired, or A and B swapped (sign reversed is fine, just flip it) | Check D2/D3; print the raw A and B. |
| Invalid-transition count climbs while running | Noise on the encoder lines, or an ISR that's too slow | Shorter encoder wires, away from motor leads; keep the ISR minimal. |
| Count changes when the motor is stopped and you touch nothing | Floating encoder inputs | `INPUT_PULLUP`; check the encoder's VCC and GND. |
| INA219 config reads `0xFFFF` or the read fails | I2C wiring, or the address isn't 0x40 (A0/A1 bridged) | Run the Sprint 1 I2C scanner (`local/sprint1/tools/i2c_scanner`). |
| Bus voltage reads 0 but the motor runs | VIN+/VIN− wired but the INA219's GND isn't tied to the supply's ground | The bus voltage is measured from VIN− to the INA219's GND. |
| Current reads negative | VIN+ and VIN− swapped | Swap them (or accept the sign, and document it). |
| Current reading is very noisy | Sampling the PWM ripple | Hardware averaging (SADC), as above. |
| Temperature reads 85.00 | Read before the first conversion finished | Request, wait 750 ms, then read. |
| Temperature reads -127.00 | No sensor: wiring or missing 4.7 kΩ pull-up | Check DQ on D4 and the pull-up to 5 V. |
| The stream stalls for about 0.75 s once per second | Blocking temperature conversion | `setWaitForConversion(false)`. |
| Motor hums but doesn't turn at low duty | Deadband (static friction) | Normal. Note the duty where it starts; you'll measure it in 2.2. |
| Motor runs at full speed whatever `PWM` you send | The ENA jumper is still on | Remove it; ENA goes to D9. |
| Fuse blows | Short in the motor leads, or a stall at high duty | Check wiring; don't hold the shaft. |
| Upload fails while the rig is connected | Serial port held by a Python script | Close the script first. |

## Resume and interview note

- "Why must the shared count be `volatile`? Why disable interrupts to read it?" (compiler caching; torn 32-bit reads on an 8-bit CPU)
- "How does quadrature give you direction?" (the order of the Gray-code states)
- "What happens if your host crashes mid-test?" (the watchdog stops the motor in 500 ms)
